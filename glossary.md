# Glossary — Every Acronym & Term in This Project

Read this once before Step 2. Terms are grouped by world, each with a plain-English explanation and *why it matters here*.

## 1. Private Equity world

- **PE (Private Equity)** — investment funds that buy companies outright (not shares on a stock market), improve them, and sell them for a profit. A "PE-backed company" is owned by such a fund. *Why here:* our target company must be PE-owned to make this a PE-style case.
- **Fund** — the pool of money a PE firm invests; raised from investors, deployed over ~5 years, then returned.
- **GP (General Partner)** — the PE firm itself; manages the fund and makes buy/sell decisions.
- **LP (Limited Partner)** — the investors whose money is in the fund (pension funds, endowments). They don't manage anything.
- **Portfolio company** — a company a PE fund owns; the whole collection is the "portfolio".
- **IC (Investment Committee)** — the senior group at a PE fund that approves investments and major interventions. Our deck is addressed to them; treat them as the most skeptical reader you'll ever write for.
- **Value Creation Plan (VCP)** — the written plan for how the fund will grow a portfolio company's value during ownership. Humatica's work always hangs off this ("fit-for-purpose org to deliver the value growth plan").
- **Buyout / LBO (Leveraged Buyout)** — buying a company using a lot of debt ("leverage") that sits on the *company*, not the fund. Debt pressure is why efficiency matters so much post-buyout.
- **Due Diligence (DD)** — the deep investigation before buying a company. Flavours: financial, commercial (market), legal, and — Humatica's niche — **organizational DD** (is the management team and org fit for the plan?). Our project is a *mini organizational DD on public data*.
- **Operating Partner** — a PE-fund executive who works inside portfolio companies post-deal to improve operations; "Talent Operating Partners" specialise in people/org topics — Humatica's natural counterparts.
- **Exit / Realization** — selling the portfolio company to return cash to LPs. Humatica's stat: 97% of PE respondents say organizational issues *delay realization*.
- **IRR (Internal Rate of Return)** — the annualised % return accounting for when money goes in and out. The headline metric PE funds are judged on.
- **MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital)** — "how many times your money back": buy at £100m, sell at £300m = 3.0x MOIC.
- **Alpha** — returns above the market average. Humatica's tagline: "helping business leaders deliver alpha."
- **Third Wave (Humatica's thesis)** — PE value-creation wave 1 = financial engineering (cheap debt), wave 2 = operational excellence (cost/efficiency), wave 3 = **organizational excellence** (culture, behaviours, management processes). Citing this shows genuine interest in *their* intellectual property.

## 2. Finance & accounting

- **Revenue / Turnover** — total sales. UK accounts call it "turnover"; same thing, and Companies House filings use that word.
- **Gross profit / Gross margin** — revenue minus direct cost of delivery, and that as a % of revenue. Matters because low Rev/FTE might reflect a labour-heavy service mix, not inefficiency.
- **CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** — smoothed yearly growth: £100→£133 over 3 years = 10%/yr CAGR. Better than averaging volatile yearly growth numbers.
- **YoY (Year over Year)** — this year vs same period last year.
- **TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)** — the last 12 months of data regardless of fiscal year.
- **FTE (Full-Time Equivalent)** — employees counted as full-time units (2 half-timers = 1 FTE). UK accounts report "average number of employees" — our best public proxy for FTE.
- **Staff costs** — wages + pensions + social security, as disclosed in UK accounts.
- **Staff cost ratio** — staff costs ÷ revenue. The single most comparable org-efficiency metric in public filings.
- **Rev/FTE (Revenue per employee)** — revenue ÷ average employees. High = lean/productive; low = inefficient OR labour-heavy mix OR over-staffed — the issue tree's Branches A/B/C exist precisely to disambiguate this.
- **Median vs Mean** — median = middle value; mean = sum ÷ count. We use **median** for peer comparisons so one outlier peer doesn't distort the benchmark.
- **Percentile** — value below which X% of the group falls; the 25th percentile means a quarter of peers are below it. Our scenario anchors.
- **IQR (Interquartile Range)** — the box between the 25th and 75th percentiles = the peer group's "normal range". If the target sits inside the IQR, there's arguably no case for change.

## 3. Companies House & UK filings

- **Companies House** — the UK government registry of companies. All UK companies must file annual accounts there; most are free. Our primary data source.
- **API (Application Programming Interface)** — a machine-readable way to request data from a service. Companies House has a free public API; we use it instead of clicking PDFs.
- **SIC code (Standard Industrial Classification)** — a 5-digit code every UK company declares describing its business (e.g., 62012 = software publishing). We use it to build a like-for-like peer set.
- **Full vs abridged accounts** — small companies may file **abridged** accounts that hide revenue and balance-sheet detail; micro-entities file almost nothing. ⚠️ Step 2 rule: only select target & peers that file full accounts, or the revenue data doesn't exist.
- **Small companies regime / audit thresholds** — UK rules that changed over time, changing *what* companies disclose; a data-comparability trap we log under issue-tree Branch D.
- **Period end / FY (Financial Year)** — the year-end date the accounts cover. Companies use different dates (31 Mar vs 31 Dec), so we normalise by fiscal year.
- **Restatement** — when a company re-issues prior-year numbers, often changed. Watchlist item.

## 4. Consulting craft

- **Hypothesis-driven work** — start with a guess of the answer, then seek evidence to *kill* it. Faster than "collect everything then see". The JD literally says "support the team on hypothesis development".
- **Issue tree / logic tree** — a diagram splitting the question into sub-questions until each is answerable with data. Quality bar: **MECE**.
- **MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)** — branches don't overlap (mutually exclusive) and together cover every possibility (collectively exhaustive). The gold standard for structuring thinking.
- **Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto)** — communicate answer first, then supporting arguments, then data — an inverted pyramid. Drives our deck Slide 1 and the "Day-1 answer" drill.
- **Action title / conclusion title** — a slide title stating the finding ("Staff costs are 8pts above peer median, driven by output per head — not pay"), not the topic ("Staff cost analysis"). All 10 of our titles do this.
- **"So what?"** — every analysis must end in an implication for the decision-maker. A chart with no "so what" is decoration.
- **Case for change** — Humatica's term for the compelling, fact-based reason people must change now. Our exec summary builds it.
- **Rightsizing** — adjusting the organization's resources (not just "cutting staff") so it's fit-for-purpose for the plan. A named Humatica service; our project's spine.
- **Scale-adjusted benchmarking** — Humatica's phrase for comparing companies of similar size so small firms aren't unfairly judged against large ones. Our Branch B.
- **Management dialogues** — Humatica Rightsizing step 4: reality-check analysis with accountable managers before acting. Our slide 9 acknowledges public data must be validated this way.
- **Benchmarking** — systematic comparison against peers on defined metrics.
- **Value at stake** — the £ quantified opportunity of closing the gap. Sizing it converts "insight" into "decision".
- **Scenario modelling** — computing the answer under low/base/high assumptions so the reader sees a range, not false precision.
- **Sensitivity analysis** — testing which assumption moves the answer most (usually a 2-way table). Tells the IC where to focus debate.
- **Assumptions tab / register** — one visible place listing every assumption with its source. Non-negotiable for model credibility.
- **Executive summary** — one slide containing the entire answer: situation, complication, resolution, numbers. Written last, shown first.

## 5. Tools & tech

- **SQL (Structured Query Language)** — the language for querying tables of data. DuckDB speaks it.
- **DuckDB** — a free, single-file analytical database that runs SQL on local files (CSV/Parquet) with no server. Ideal here: light, reproducible, resume-friendly.
- **CSV / XLSX** — comma-separated file / Excel workbook formats.
- **README** — the front-page documentation of a code/data folder. Quality bar: a stranger rebuilds the model from ours.
- **Git / GitHub** — version control / public hosting. The repo link goes on the CV *after* Step 6.
- **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** — an AI pattern where the tool first *retrieves* relevant source documents, then generates an answer *with citations to them*. Our optional Step 7 accelerator.
- **Citation** — pointing back to the exact source of a claim. The AI layer's whole credibility rests on this.
